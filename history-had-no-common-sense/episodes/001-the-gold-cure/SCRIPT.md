# Episode 001 — The Gold Cure

- **Format:** YouTube Short, 9:16 vertical (1080×1920), 30 fps
- **Runtime:** ~1:34
- **Word count:** ~250 (≈150 wpm, dry conversational delivery)
- **Working title:** *The $379 Million Cure That Contained No Medicine*
- **Alt titles:** *They Injected Gold to Cure Drinking. There Was No Gold.* /
  *America's Favorite Cure for Alcoholism Was Fake. He Was Still Right.*

Delivery note: flat and unhurried. The numbers do the work — the host never
sells them. The only warmth in the whole read arrives at "showing up for
each other," and it should be underplayed, not swelled.

---

## [0:00–0:06] HOOK

> In the 1890s, the most popular cure for alcoholism in America was gold
> injections.
>
> There was no gold in them.

## [0:06–0:24] THE PITCH

> Dwight, Illinois. Dr. Leslie Keeley hangs out a sign with a promise on it:
> "Alcoholism is a disease, and I can cure it."
>
> Four injections a day. A tonic every two hours. Four weeks, and you go home
> fixed.
>
> The secret ingredient — bichloride of gold.

## [0:24–0:42] THE SCALE

> And people came.
>
> Two hundred institutes. Every state. Canada, Mexico, then Europe.
>
> One year, Keeley cleared one-point-four million dollars.
>
> Adjusted for today — three hundred seventy-nine million.

## [0:42–0:56] THE DEBUNK

> Then the chemists showed up.
>
> Turns out "bichloride of gold" isn't a compound. It isn't anything. The
> name is chemically meaningless.
>
> They tested the medicine. Gold content: zero.

## [0:56–1:18] THE TURN

> So. Open and shut. Snake oil.
>
> Except.
>
> In 1880, the going wisdom was that drunks were sinners. Weak. A character
> defect you could pray off.
>
> Keeley said it was a disease. He was lying about the gold — but on that,
> he was decades ahead of everybody.
>
> And then his patients did something he never sold them. They started
> showing up for each other. Keeley Leagues. Thirty thousand members, three
> hundred seventy chapters. People who'd gotten out, sitting in rooms with
> people who were still in it.

## [1:18–1:34] PAYOFF

> 1935. Alcoholics Anonymous gets founded on those two ideas exactly: it's a
> disease, and nobody does it alone.
>
> The gold did nothing.
>
> The cure was never in the syringe. It was in the waiting room.

## Revision log

- **V1** — straight chronology, ended on the fraud. Dead. It was a scam
  story with no reason to exist, and it punched down at the patients.
- **V2** — moved the debunk earlier and added the "Except." turn. Better,
  but the ending moralized ("maybe we're not so different today") — cut.
- **V3 (this)** — hook now front-loads the contradiction inside 6 seconds.
  Payoff is concrete rather than a lesson: *syringe vs. waiting room*. The
  host never states the moral, the image carries it.

## Caption / description draft

> In the 1890s, over 200 clinics across America sold injections of
> "bichloride of gold" as a cure for alcoholism. The compound doesn't exist.
> The medicine contained no gold. Dr. Leslie Keeley made the equivalent of
> $379 million.
>
> But he insisted addiction was a disease, not a moral failing — decades
> before medicine agreed. And his patients invented peer support groups on
> their own: 30,000 people, 370 chapters. Forty years later, AA was founded
> on those same two ideas.
